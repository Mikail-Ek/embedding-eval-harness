Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) is a technique that gives large language models access to external, up-to-date information at the moment they answer a question, rather than relying only on what they learned during training. This solves two core limitations of language models. First, a model's knowledge is frozen at its training cutoff date, since training a large model is slow and expensive and is not done continuously. After that cutoff, the model has no way of knowing about anything that happened afterward. Second, models have no access to private or internal data, such as a company's own documents, unless that information is explicitly provided to them.

RAG works in three steps, which is where the name comes from. First, retrieval: the system takes a user's question, converts it into a vector representation, and searches a knowledge base for the most relevant pieces of text. Second, augmentation: those retrieved pieces of text are inserted into the prompt as additional context. Third, generation: the language model produces an answer using that retrieved context, rather than relying purely on its internal memory.

A major benefit of this approach is that it reduces hallucination, since the model's answers are grounded in real, retrieved source material rather than only its training data. It also means that keeping a system up to date is much cheaper than retraining an entire model. Instead, only the external knowledge base needs to be updated whenever new information becomes available. This makes RAG a practical and widely used pattern for building AI systems that need access to current or proprietary information.
